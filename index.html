Context

What the engine does today

Troopod's CRO engine audits a client's ecommerce site against hundreds of checks. Each one is a precise check with a defined pass/fail condition, such as "a size guide is visible above the fold on the PDP". Each check is tied to a reason shoppers don't convert, such as low trust or unclear information. Which checks apply depends on the brand's category and product type. A jewellery brand and a supplement brand are not audited against the same list.

Simplified, an audit runs like this:

Brand contextCategory, price point, AOV, funnel numbers, customer objections
→
AI picks checksChooses the checks relevant to this brand
→
Page checksEach check passes, fails or doesn't apply
→
DiagnosisFailures grouped and ranked by severity
→
AI drafts fixesOne fix per failing check
→
Consultant reviewPrioritised roadmap for the client
Evidence layer (the part you're designing)Live GA4 + Clarity data matched to pages and checks, compared with Supabase history. It feeds three steps: which checks to run, how failures are ranked, and which fix the AI recommends.

A CRO consultant reviews every AI recommendation before it reaches the client. We rerun the audit every few weeks to measure what changed. Supabase stores the check library, past audits, category benchmarks and the results of fixes we've tested before.

The problem

A failed check doesn't prove a lost sale

The checks are objective, but they describe what a page has, not how shoppers behave on it. Funnel numbers and session-recording insights reach us today as one-off snapshots, mostly at onboarding, rather than as live data tied to specific pages and checks. That causes three precision problems:

  • False positives. A PDP can fail a check and still convert well. We report a gap that isn't costing the client anything.
  • Ranking from assumptions. Severity and brand context decide the order. Neither knows that this exact page gets 40% of mobile traffic and loses most of it before add-to-cart.
  • Fixes not tied to behaviour. The AI picks a fix from the brand's context and past results. It can't yet see where shoppers rage-click or how far they scroll on the page in question.

Definition of precision for this brief

Of the gaps the engine marks as high priority, the share that are real conversion leaks. A leak is confirmed by behaviour data, by a CRO strategist's review, or by a past experiment that won when we fixed the same gap.

The ask

Design the evidence layer

Plan a layer that keeps live behaviour data next to the audit and hands it to the AI agents at the right moments. It should:

  1. Find the funnel stage with the biggest drop-off (browse to cart, cart to checkout, or checkout to purchase) from GA4, compared with category benchmarks. That tells the engine where to focus.
  2. Pull the GA4 and Clarity data for the pages and devices each check covers.
  3. For each failing check, decide whether the behaviour data supports it, contradicts it, or is too thin to say.
  4. Look up Supabase for benchmarks from similar brands and past results on the same check.
  5. Give the AI agents a structured evidence pack, so the ranking and the recommended fix are specific, cite their sources and are easy for a consultant to review.

02

Five checks mapped to signals

Example: "Add-to-cart button below the fold on mobile" → mobile view_item→add_to_cart rate against the desktop rate and the category benchmark, plus mobile scroll depth on PDPs.

03

How you'd re-rank failing checks

A scoring idea that uses the evidence. At minimum it should weigh how severe the gap is, how much traffic it affects, whether it sits in the worst funnel stage, how strong the behavioural evidence is, and how often fixing it has worked before. Explain how the weights would be tuned as results come in from each audit cycle.

04

How the AI uses the evidence

What goes into the evidence pack, how the AI returns structured output (the format below is a starting point), and how it marks a finding "insufficient evidence" instead of guessing.

05

How you'd prove it worked

06

What you'd build first

Things we'll ask about

Be ready to discuss these

  1. A page has a clear gap but too little traffic to judge. What does the engine report, and how does the client see it?
  2. GA4 says a page converts well but Clarity shows heavy rage clicks there. How do you settle the conflict?
  3. Clients tag events and name page paths differently. How do you map their GA4 data onto our page types without hand work for every client?
  4. How do you stop the LLM from quoting a number that isn't in the evidence pack?
  5. We rerun the audit every few weeks. How does your layer show whether a shipped fix moved the numbers, and feed that back into future rankings?
  6. What would you cache, and what does one client audit cost in API calls and LLM tokens?
  7. Clients grant read access to their analytics. What scopes do you request, where do tokens live, and what happens when access is revoked?
